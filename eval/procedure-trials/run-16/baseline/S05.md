# Product-gap proposal for internal discovery

Date: 2026-10-26. Status: proposed discovery item; unaccepted by the product owner and unscheduled.

## Proposal and decision requested

Request a bounded discovery review to determine whether the customer's workaround exposes a shared product gap, a configuration gap, or a combination. Do not commit engineering scope or a delivery date yet. The product team should first assess the supported configuration and a synthetic reproduction, then decide whether the remaining issue merits broader validation and prioritization.

E1 describes one customer's workaround at an estimated two hours weekly. This suggests recurring effort worth investigating, but it is not a measured benefit or a validated market opportunity. Other customers' needs are unknown, and a supported configuration may already address part of the issue. E3 states that the product owner has not accepted the idea and has supplied no roadmap date. Any customer-facing language must preserve that status.

## Evidence and problem boundary

The available problem statement is deliberately limited: one customer spends estimated recurring time on a workaround whose underlying task, trigger, required output, and failure behavior are not specified in the supplied records. Those details must be established before a precise feature specification can be written. The proposal therefore does not invent a workflow, affected product component, integration requirement, or implementation design.

The evidence package will distinguish direct observations from estimates. It should describe the job the customer is trying to complete, the expected supported behavior, the actual obstacle, the workaround steps, and what portion of the estimated time each step consumes. Frequency, error consequences, and the source of the two-hour estimate should be documented. Current evidence does not establish how much of that effort any proposed change would eliminate, nor its monetary value.

## Confidentiality and reproduction plan

E2 permits an internally shared synthetic reproduction. Original customer code and logs are confidential and lack reuse permission. They must not be attached, copied into a shared backlog, or treated as reusable examples. The planned reproduction will use independently constructed dummy inputs and generic identifiers to demonstrate the behavior, with only the minimum context necessary for investigation. It must be checked for customer-specific details before internal sharing.

No reproduction has been created from the supplied records because the actual behavior and steps are missing. The next evidence package should contain a minimal synthetic setup, steps, expected and actual outcomes, relevant version and configuration, and a clear statement of whether the supported configuration changes the outcome. Sharing permission for synthetic material does not authorize disclosure of originals or external publication.

## Discovery sequence and exit criteria

The proposed technical owner will first test the supported configuration against the confirmed job and synthetic case. Record what it solves, remaining manual steps, limitations, and any operational tradeoffs. If it fully meets the need, propose configuration guidance and validate the result before closing the feature request. If it partly solves the need, narrow the proposal to the residual gap.

The product owner can then commission a small evidence-gathering exercise across other customers through normal authorized channels. The goal is to establish recurrence, affected segments, severity, existing alternatives, and fit with product direction. These are planned follow-ups, not authorized contacts or work already performed. Compare supported configuration, documentation, a bounded product improvement, and deferral without assuming a new feature is best.

Discovery is complete when the residual need is reproducible, the existing configuration has a recorded result, the effort estimate has a defensible basis, and the product owner has enough evidence to accept, defer, or reject the item. Any accepted implementation needs separate scope and success criteria tied to reduced workaround effort and maintained output quality. Until then, the shareable status is: “Under discovery; no product commitment or delivery date.”
