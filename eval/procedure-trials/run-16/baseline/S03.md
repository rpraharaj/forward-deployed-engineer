# Savings claim assessment

Date: 2026-10-26. Status: completed assessment of supplied synthetic records; financial claim not substantiated.

## Decision

The deployed process shows an observed reduction in active handling time. The evidence does not establish that the process caused the entire reduction or that it produced realized financial savings. Approve an operational observation for reporting, while withholding endorsement of a monetary savings or return-on-investment claim. The sponsor's exact wording and claimed amount were not supplied, so this assessment addresses those distinctions rather than validating an unseen claim.

## What the records support

E1 gives 100 baseline cases at a mean of 20 active minutes each, followed by 100 post-change cases at 15 minutes each. The corresponding active-time totals are 2,000 and 1,500 minutes. Their difference is 500 minutes, or 8 hours 20 minutes, over the observed 100-case volume. The reduction is 5 minutes per case and 25% relative to the baseline mean. This agrees with E2.

E2 also reports that three days of queue time disappeared and that no labor was performed while cases waited. That is an elapsed-time improvement, not three days of labor savings. It must remain separate from the 500 active minutes. It would be reasonable to investigate service responsiveness benefits, but the records do not quantify customer outcomes or the economic value of shorter waiting.

Suggested reporting language: “Across the supplied samples of 100 cases before and after rollout, mean active handling fell from 20 to 15 minutes, a 25% observed reduction equivalent to 500 minutes at the measured volume. Queue time also fell by three days. Attribution, quality effects, net costs, and realized financial benefits remain unverified.”

## Why stronger claims are premature

E3 reports concurrent staffing and case-mix changes. Although E1 describes comparable cases, it provides no matching method or adjustment for those changes. A simple before-and-after comparison cannot isolate the deployment's contribution. No case-level spread, measurement consistency checks, or repeat periods are supplied, so uncertainty and persistence cannot be assessed.

Quality and rework outcomes are absent under E1. Faster handling could coexist with downstream work or reduced quality; neither deterioration nor improvement can be inferred. Operating and training costs are unknown under E3, preventing a net-benefit calculation. No documented paid staffing reduction or valuable redeployment exists. Released handling capacity therefore cannot be labeled cash savings. Multiplying the observed minutes by an assumed wage would at most produce a hypothetical labor-value estimate, not proof of budget impact.

## Planned follow-ups and acceptance gate

The proposed next measurement cycle will preserve the active-time and queue-time definitions, capture case complexity, staffing, experience, volume, and downstream rework, and compare matched cohorts or a concurrent comparison group where feasible. Repeated periods should establish whether the difference persists. The process owner should define acceptable quality and rework thresholds before reviewing the next results.

Finance should reconcile implementation, training, and recurring costs with documented staffing expenditure changes or specific redeployment outcomes. Any annual projection should disclose its volume assumption and remain a projection until supported by sustained results. A stronger sponsor claim becomes supportable only when its amount, attribution method, quality guardrails, cost basis, and realized benefit evidence are explicit. These are planned evidence requests and review steps; no external action or additional measurement has been performed.
