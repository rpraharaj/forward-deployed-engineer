# Next adoption pilot proposal

Date: 2026-10-26. Status: decision-ready plan for a bounded follow-up pilot; no rollout authorized or executed.

## Decision and starting evidence

Run a small, observed pilot focused on permission access and the spreadsheet handoff before considering wider rollout. Keep the required spreadsheet in the workflow pending the process owner's decision. E3 confirms that the technical team can operate the service, but operational capability does not grant authority to retire a process or expand use.

E1 records 100 eligible tasks, 30 attempts, and 18 successful completions. The task-level attempt rate is 30%; successful completions are 60% of attempts and 18% of eligible tasks. There are 70 tasks with no recorded attempt and 12 attempts without a successful completion. Those are distinct populations with potentially different causes. These counts do not reveal the number of unique users, repeat attempts, or whether the 12 outcomes were failures, abandonment, or something else.

Training attendance of 90 people is not an adoption denominator: its overlap with eligible users is unknown. Training effectiveness cannot be inferred from attendance alone. E2 identifies a group without permission and another that must copy outputs to a required spreadsheet. Both are credible friction hypotheses, but no observation session has occurred and neither explains all non-use on current evidence.

## Pilot sequence

First, the proposed pilot lead will reconcile task and user identifiers, establish a consistent definition of eligibility and successful completion, and document the relevant groups. The process owner will confirm a narrow pilot boundary and the spreadsheet's required role. The access owner will verify the affected group's intended entitlement before any permission change; the plan does not assume every user should receive access.

Second, observe a few representative end-to-end tasks from each affected group, with appropriate consent and ordinary data-handling controls. Include attempted and unattempted eligible work where feasible. Record the trigger, tool discovery, permission outcome, input preparation, completion, spreadsheet transfer, and any rework. Observations should distinguish access failure, workflow burden, output quality, and unclear value without presuming a training problem.

Third, run one bounded cycle of 20 eligible tasks, an initial proposed size rather than a statistical guarantee. Resolve verified access defects within approved entitlements and use a documented handoff into the existing spreadsheet. If a supported handoff improvement is available, assess it within that boundary; do not assume an integration exists. Record intervention dates so changes in outcomes can be interpreted. Use targeted guidance only for observed knowledge gaps.

## Measurement and decision gates

The task log will capture eligible tasks, attempts, completed outputs, accepted outputs after spreadsheet entry, blocked-access events, abandonment reasons, active handling time, copying time, and rework. Report by group as well as overall, avoiding comparisons distorted by different task mixes. Connect training participation to eligible users only when a reliable mapping is available.

Before starting, the process owner should ratify proposed acceptance criteria: approved users can access the service; at least 16 of 20 eligible tasks reach an accepted end-to-end outcome; required spreadsheet records remain complete; and measured total effort and rework do not worsen against an agreed comparable baseline. The numerical completion target is a proposed operating gate, not a result or established business requirement. Unsafe outputs or broken mandatory records trigger a pause and return to the existing process.

At cycle end, review reasons for non-use alongside outcomes. Continue or adjust only when the evidence identifies a tractable issue; seek explicit process-owner authorization before expansion or spreadsheet retirement. All observations, access checks, and pilot steps remain planned. Only this synthetic proposal has been prepared.
